DEAD DROP — READ THIS FIRST

5 minutes, both players · paper playtest of an asynchronous hidden-movement game · ruleset 1.0.0-alpha.1 · map_alpha v1.0.0 · No homework required — everything you need is on your own sheet.

The game in four sentences

Dead Drop is hide-and-seek in a city of 48 locations over a case that lasts 8 days. One of you is the Fugitive: moving in secret, collecting two public "drops" in order, trying to reach the exit gate — or simply never be found. The other is the Investigator: you never see the Fugitive, only fragments of evidence the rules hand you, and you win by being one step ahead of where they are. There are no dice and no luck: given the moves, the outcome is pure logic. Today's paper table runs on a 2-minute clock standing in for the online game's 24-hour deadlines.

Who wins

FUGITIVE wins by ESCAPE — end any day standing on the gate with both drops collected, in order (drop 1 before drop 2). If Day 8 ends and you were never captured: bad news — that verdict is EXPIRY and it counts for the Investigator. Your real deadline is your stamina: every move costs SP (Foot 1 · Tram 2 · Metro 3), Holds cost 1 (max 2 per case), and at 0 SP you are stranded (a free forced Hold — no penalty, but you're a statue).
INVESTIGATOR wins by CAPTURE or EXPIRY — a stakeout (3 IP) armed today catches the Fugitive tomorrow if they end that day on your node; otherwise it misses silently and is spent. Probes (1 IP, ≤2/day) ask "are you here today?" and never capture — they pin. Blind probing cannot win: the scheduled evidence — district reveals on Days 3 & 6, transit class on Day 4 — is your real map. Unspent IP carries over; Day 8 forbids new stakeouts.

The shape of every day (always the same 7 steps, in this order)

  1. Fugitive acts — in secret: one move along an edge, or a Hold. Write it on your route sheet; announce nothing yet.
  2. Stakeout check — only if the Investigator armed one yesterday (exact script on the Day Mat).
  3. Reveals fire — Days 3 & 6: which district they ended in. Day 4: the class of that day's move, or "no movement".
  4. Drop check — if they end on the pending drop site, it completes publicly (site + day). Standing on drop 2 before drop 1 does nothing.
  5. Escape check — both drops collected and on the gate: they win on the spot.
  6. Investigator acts — up to 2 probes (1 IP) and/or 1 stakeout (3 IP), or wait. Written in private on your own sheet.
  7. Clock — Day 8 ending here = EXPIRY, Investigator wins. Otherwise next day begins.

How we talk at the table (the whole etiquette — 5 lines)

Fugitive, out loudOnly what the rules make public: district reveals (Days 3/6), your Day-4 transit class or "no movement", drop completions ("Drop 1 completed at D2-06"), "captured" if it happens, and the truthful answer to a stakeout question. Never show the route sheet until the case ends — then everything is on the table (that's a rule: full logs reveal at case end).
Investigator, out loud"Investigator phase" when you act — then write probes/stakeouts on your own private sheet. Placements and results are yours alone until the case ends. Nothing else you do is announced.
The stakeout questionWhen a stakeout is armed, the Investigator asks once, out loud: "Stakeout check — did your day end on [node]?" Answer truthfully: yes = CAPTURE, no = nothing happens. (A "no" tells you one node that was watched — a paper-table artifact the online game doesn't have; it gets logged once on the dispute sheet. Don't build strategy on it.)
DisputesAny rules argument → one row on the dispute sheet, play the provisional ruling, never argue past 3 minutes. Table rulings are provisional by design; the rules doc gets patched only after the session.
The clockYour opponent times you: 2 minutes per Fugitive action, 2 minutes per Investigator phase. Time out = auto-Hold (Fugitive; free "Stranded" if 0 SP) or auto-wait (Investigator) — just log it in Notes. (Online, two consecutive missed 24-h deadlines forfeit the case; paper skips that bookkeeping.)

Before day 1 — 60 seconds

  1. The facilitator reads the case config aloud; copy it onto the public Case Record sheet.
  2. Fugitive takes the Fugitive Sheet (hidden) + a pencil. Investigator takes the Investigator Sheet — keep it private.
  3. The map goes where both can reach it. The Day Mat sits in the middle; glance at it every step until the flow feels natural.
